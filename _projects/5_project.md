V<sub>2</sub>Se<sub>9</sub> displays facile electrochemical insertion of up to 1.6 Mg<sup>2+</sup> per unit formula with fast diffusion (coefficients of 10<sup>−10</sup>–10<sup>−12</sup> cm<sup>2</sup> s<sup>−1</sup>) surpassing best-in-class materials like Mo<sub>6</sub>S<sub>8</sub>. Detailed structural characterization of synchrotron X-ray diffraction data with ab initio Maximum Entropy Method analysis reveals Mg<sup>2+</sup> insertion into octahedral sites within the large vdW space between [V<sub>4/sub>Se<sub>18</sub>] chains. Fast rate performance is attributed to low structural perturbation and low diffusion barriers, calculated by bond valence pathway analysis, resulting from the low charge-per-size of anionic selenium. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy and X-ray absorption spectroscopy reveal that reversible insertion of Mg<sup>2+</sup>+ is facilitated by V<sup>5+</sup>/V<sup>3+</sup> redox. V<sub>2</sub>Se<sub>9</sub> demonstrates that selenides, despite their larger molecular weight, offer potential as fast-rate positive electrode materials for magnesium batteries over well-explored oxides and sulfides.

You can read more about this in out paper in [J Mat Chem A](hhttps://doi.org/10.1039/d4ta04469j)
